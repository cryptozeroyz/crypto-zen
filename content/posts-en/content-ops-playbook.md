A lot of people ask me how I grew my Twitter account. This post is a full review of what I did over the past year — only the reusable lessons, no chicken soup.

## 1. Content Mix: Three Legs to Stand On

After watching many similar accounts, I settled on a 5:3:2 structure:

- **50% substance**: on-chain data breakdowns, tool tutorials, and methodology. This content grows followers the slowest, but it defines the account's professional image;
- **30% opinions**: independent takes on market events. Mistakes are allowed, but the logic must be consistent;
- **20% personality**: daily life, failures, and the occasional shitpost. This drives the highest engagement and turns followers into "one of us."

An account that only posts substance reads like a research report — no personality. An account that only posts jokes is noise — nothing sticks. An imbalanced ratio is the number one reason most accounts fail to grow.

## 2. Publishing Cadence: Consistency Beats Spikes

My rhythm: one to two original tweets a day, plus one long-form post per week (the content on this site). A few details:

1. **Post at fixed times**: crypto readers are most active between 8–10am and 9–11pm (Beijing time); long-form posts go out on Tuesdays and Thursdays;
2. **Threads perform 3x better than single tweets**, but I do no more than two per week to avoid burning reader attention;
3. **Review the data on Sundays**: pull impressions, engagement, and follower growth for the week, find the best-performing post, analyze why it worked, and replicate it.

## 3. Three Mistakes I Made

- **The hot-topic trap**: early on I spent a lot of time chasing trending topics. Individual posts performed well, but follower conversion was terrible — the followers I gained weren't the right audience. I now keep hot-topic content under 10%;
- **The engagement-pod trap**: mutual promotion with other accounts brought short-term growth, but the algorithm quickly flagged the low-quality interaction and my account's reach actually dropped;
- **The metrics-anxiety trap**: for two months I stared at the follower count every day, and my content became increasingly bland. Moving the review to a weekly cadence noticeably improved my topic selection.

## 4. The Single Most Important Insight

**Account growth is compound interest on trust.** Followers from a single viral post are mostly foam — only consistently useful content turns followers into an audience.

That's also why I insist on publishing long-form posts on my own website instead of only on Twitter: the platform is rented; the content assets must be your own.
