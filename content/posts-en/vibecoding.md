I, a product operations specialist with absolutely no development skills, built a personal website from scratch in two weeks, relying entirely on AI to write the code. Now it runs on my own server, has its own domain name, and is still being iterated every day.

The starting point two weeks ago: Vercel Free Plan + asking for tutorials everywhere. Now, two weeks later:

1. My own server, located in a data center in Seoul, costs 68 per year. 2. My own domain name, cryptozen.fun. 3. Three content sections: research reports, essays, and operations, plus my memecoin watchlist. 4. Integrated with Trench Index, automatically generating article background images. The backend supports direct import of Word and Notion documents. 5. Visitors can send flowers, and each flower is recorded on GitHub, making it more transparent than some cryptocurrencies. 6. Daily fortune cookie, with 48 Degen fortunes. The probability of getting a "super lucky" fortune is 15%. 7. Fully automated HTTPS. Code pushes trigger automatic deployment on both sites.

For the above features, not a single line of code was written by me. I only did two things throughout the entire process: **acting as the product manager and clicking "Confirm."**

## What AI is changing is not writing code, but "who can make products"

In the past, people like me were excluded from "making products." I had plenty of ideas, but they all got stuck at the implementation stage because I couldn't write code. Now, that bottleneck is gone: I verbally describe the requirements, the AI handles the implementation, and I'm responsible for acceptance, suggesting modifications, and then accepting again.

Therefore, the essence of this wave of AI programming is not "improved programmer efficiency," but rather **the collapse of the barriers to production methods**. When everyone can create a product just by speaking, what becomes scarce is no longer the ability to execute, but rather ideas, taste, and the ability to judge "what is worth doing."

## But to be honest, most vibe coding currently only satisfies emotional value.

The greatest value of this site is that it makes me happy. I built a temple. It would be great if people came to offer incense. If not, I'll just consider it a home for my own meme research.

This is also my biggest question about this wave of AI programming: Productivity has indeed increased, but most of the output remains at the level of personal emotional value. How can we make a profit, and how can we generate real value? I don't have an answer to this question, and I'm **a bit confused**, but I think everyone who creates their first work using AI should think about it.

## More important than efficiency is the fact that organizational relationships are bound to change.

AI is not only a change in the mode of production; there must also be a change in organizational relationships.

In the past, creating a product required a product team, a design team, a front-end team, and a back-end team. Now, one person plus AI constitutes the entire team. When "one person = one team" becomes a reality, the boundaries of the company, the way people collaborate, and the skills that are valued will all be reshaped.

So, my advice is: use AI, leverage AI, no matter what. Not to catch up with anyone, but to be personally immersed in the change, rather than standing on the sidelines and watching.
