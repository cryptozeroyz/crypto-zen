This is the first piece in my quarterly memecoin blue-chip review series. I picked PEPE not because it's the most exciting, but because its holder structure is the most typical: early whales, market makers, retail, and a class of "slow-accumulation" addresses whose share keeps rising.

## 1. Top-Down: Where Is the Floating Supply Concentrating?

The conclusion first: **over the past 90 days, the combined holdings of PEPE's top 50 addresses fell from 38.4% to 33.1%**. Read naively, that suggests "whales are dumping." But break the structure down and the story is completely different.

| Address type | 90 days ago | Today | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early whales (2023 cohort) | 21.7% | 18.2% | -3.5% |
| Suspected market makers | 6.8% | 4.9% | -1.9% |
| CEX hot wallets | 9.9% | 10.0% | +0.1% |
| Slow-accumulation addresses | 5.4% | 8.7% | +3.3% |

The real incremental buyer is that fourth category.

## 2. Three Traits of "Slow-Accumulation" Addresses

We classified 200+ addresses that kept buying small amounts over 90 days, never sold, and funded through compliant channels as "slow-accumulation" addresses. Their behavior is remarkably consistent:

1. **Steady buying rhythm**: two to three purchases a week, median size around $12K, never chasing pumps;
2. **Withdrawals to cold wallets**: funds leave the exchange within 24 hours;
3. **Capital overlap with known smart-money wallets**: about 31% of these addresses have received transfers from known smart-money addresses.

This pattern doesn't look retail — it looks like institutions or high-net-worth individuals executing a fixed allocation plan.

## 3. Characterizing the Whale Distribution

The early-whale share fell 3.5 percentage points; we need to distinguish "active dumping" from "passive fade." We checked how the top 20 distributing addresses sold:

- 14 sold via OTC desks (visible OTC escrow-contract interactions) — **not a single transaction hit the open market**;
- 5 transferred to Coinbase / Kraken, but at a gentle pace — no more than 8% of holdings per week;
- Only 1 showed clear panic selling.

**Conclusion: this round of distribution looks like natural profit-taking by early winners, not a trend-level bearish signal.** See the chart below — price held a wide range all quarter, confirming a handoff of chips rather than one-sided exit.

## 4. Risks and a Tracking Checklist

This report is not investment advice. Three signals to keep tracking:

- Whether slow-accumulation addresses can push past 10% (further structure improvement);
- Whether market-maker inventory keeps shrinking (too low and short-term volatility rises);
- Whether a second case of panic selling appears among early whales.
