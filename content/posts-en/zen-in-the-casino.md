A friend asked me: you keep preaching DYOR, yet you buy memecoins yourself — isn't that contradictory?

It's not. I accepted a long time ago that **the memecoin market is essentially a casino**. My job isn't to pretend it doesn't exist, but to stay clear-headed inside it. This post is about the three mindsets I've settled into.

## Mindset One: Split Your Portfolio into "Conviction" and "Entertainment"

My wallet has always held two kinds of positions, roughly at an 8:2 ratio.

- **Conviction positions**: BTC, ETH — assets I plan to hold for five-plus years. A 50% drawdown doesn't cost me a night's sleep;
- **Entertainment positions**: everything memecoin goes here. The moment I buy, I treat the money as already gone — the rest is just watching what drama unfolds.

The biggest benefit: **it kills 80% of your emotional volatility**. When a dog coin goes to zero, you don't suffer, because you never expected anything. If it does a 10x, that's an unexpected comedy.

## Mindset Two: Don't Predict, Respond

I've watched too many people try to predict "the next PEPE," then declare the market broken after their hundredth failure.

My approach is completely different: I don't predict, I respond. Specifically, I write three rules in advance:

1. No single memecoin exceeds 20% of my entertainment allocation;
2. If market cap drops below a pre-written level, I exit unconditionally — no averaging down;
3. After a 5x gain, I pull out my principal in batches and let the profits run.

**Discipline doesn't make you more money — it keeps you alive long enough.** Staying alive is the only way to catch the wave that's actually meant for you.

## Mindset Three: Accept That Missing Out Is the Norm

> In the memecoin market, every pump you miss is a pump someone else is riding. That ledger never balances — so stop keeping it.

Last year I saw a coin at a $2M market cap and didn't buy. Three months later it hit $800M. I felt bad for about two days, then figured something out: with my position sizing, even if I'd bought, I'd only have made a small slice of my entertainment allocation — **that "missed $800M" was never mine to begin with, just like a lottery jackpot is never mine**.

After that realization, I check charts far less often and sleep much better.

## Epilogue

The most fascinating thing about memecoins isn't that they can make you rich — it's that they amplify humanity's rawest greed and fear, display it on-chain in real time, and let anyone inspect it.

To me, that's a free behavioral-finance laboratory. Losses are tuition, gains are bonuses — and either way, I go home with a pocketful of interesting observations to write about.

That's what "practicing zen in the casino" means: **in the game, but outside the frame.**
